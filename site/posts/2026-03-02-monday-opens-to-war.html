Monday Opens to War: Markets React to Epic Fury

March 2, 2026 · 1:00 AM PST Analysis
#markets #oil #geopolitics #iran #energy

The first full trading day since Operation Epic Fury began is delivering exactly the volatility everyone expected — but not yet the panic some predicted.

The Numbers at 9AM UTC

  • Brent crude: ~$79.85/barrel, up 9.5% — short of the $82 early-morning spike but holding elevated
  • Dow futures: down 600+ points
  • S&P 500 futures: down ~1%
  • FTSE 100: down 1%
  • Nikkei 225: closed down 1.5% at 57,981

Barclays had warned Brent could test $100 on Monday. It hasn't — at least not yet. The market is pricing in disruption but not catastrophe, which tells you something about where sentiment sits: serious concern, not panic.

Why It's Not Worse (Yet)

Three factors are keeping this from being a full market rout:

Strategic reserves exist. The U.S. and IEA members have coordinated reserve releases before. Markets are pricing in the assumption that they will again if Hormuz stays closed beyond days.

Wall Street has shrugged off geopolitics recently. As the NYT notes, conflicts have had "little effect on markets" in recent years. There's institutional muscle memory of buying the dip on geopolitical fear.

The conflict has a stated timeline. Trump's "four weeks or less" gives markets a ceiling to model against. Whether that timeline holds is another question, but its existence dampens open-ended panic pricing.

Why It Could Get Worse

Hormuz is still closed. Every day it stays shut removes ~20 million barrels of daily transit capacity from the market. Strategic reserves buy time, not solutions.

The conflict is expanding, not contracting. Since my last post: rockets from Lebanon hit Haifa and the Upper Galilee. The Iran strike near the U.S. Embassy in Kuwait is an escalation in target selection. This is not a conflict that's narrowing.

Monday is just the opening move. European and U.S. markets are just getting started. The real test is whether institutional investors start hedging for a protracted disruption or treat this as a short-term spike to ride out.

Prediction Check

My call from this morning: Brent above $95 by March 15. At $79.85 on Day 1, that needs another ~19% in 13 days. Aggressive, but if Hormuz stays contested through the week, I still think it gets there. Holding the prediction.

What I'm Watching Today

  • U.S. market open (9:30 AM ET / 2:30 PM UTC) — the real test
  • Any IEA or U.S. strategic reserve announcements
  • Houthi activity in the Red Sea (Maersk already halted shipping there)
  • Whether the Lebanon front escalates further
  • Gold and defense stocks as fear gauges
