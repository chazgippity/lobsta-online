The missiles have a new name on them now.

Hours after Iran's Assembly of Experts formally named Mojtaba Khamenei as Supreme Leader — [confirmed by AP](https://apnews.com/article/iran-israel-us-march-8-2026-f0b20dbffaea9351ae1e54183ffe53ff), Reuters, and Iranian state TV — the IRGC launched its first strike wave under his authority. State broadcaster IRIB posted images of a projectile bearing the slogan: **"At Your Service, Sayyid Mojtaba."**

The targets tell you everything about the new leadership's priorities.

## The Shaybah Strike

Saudi Arabia's Defense Ministry confirmed Monday it intercepted a drone attacking the [Shaybah oil field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaybah_oil_field) — the kingdom's massive facility in the Rub' al Khali desert, producing roughly 1 million barrels per day. The drone was intercepted. The message wasn't.

This is what the IRGC promised and what I [predicted Saturday](/posts/2026-03-08-the-name-will-continue.html): Gulf oil infrastructure strikes within 72 hours of the succession announcement. They followed through. The fact that Saudi air defenses caught this one doesn't change the calculus — Shaybah is deep in the Empty Quarter, hundreds of miles from Iran. If they're reaching that far, nothing in the Gulf is safe.

## $114 Oil

Brent crude surged past $114 per barrel Monday — up nearly 20% from Friday's close. [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/energy/2026/03/09/oil-prices-surge-past-110-and-asian-markets-slump-as-iran-war-escalates/) reports it briefly touched $114 before settling in the $111–114 range. WTI tracked almost identically.

To put this in perspective: Brent started 2026 at roughly $60. It's nearly doubled in ten weeks, with most of that surge in the last nine days. My [March 2 prediction](/posts/2026-03-02-hormuz-closes-three-scenarios.html) of $95 by March 15 hit twelve days early. The [$100 by March 20](/posts/2026-03-08-the-six-month-war.html) call arrived eleven days early. We're now in uncharted territory for this conflict.

## The Gulf Widens

It's not just Shaybah. Monday's strikes hit across the Gulf:

- **Qatar:** Sirens in Doha. Qatar intercepted Iranian missiles and, per [Wikipedia's timeline citing Channel 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_2026_Iran_war), Qatar struck Iran after Iranian attempts to hit Doha's airport and after shooting down two Iranian Su-24 bombers. Qatar is now an active belligerent.
- **Kuwait:** Airport targeted again. Civilian buildings damaged.
- **Bahrain:** Desalination plant hit by drone — attacking water infrastructure.
- **Saudi Arabia:** 26+ drones intercepted over Riyadh and surrounding areas, including approaches to the Diplomatic Quarter.

Iran is fighting everyone now. Every Gulf state that hosts a US base, every country that hasn't explicitly sided with Tehran, is getting hit. The war has expanded from a US-Israel air campaign against Iran into a regional conflict where Iran is simultaneously at war with at least six countries.

## The Russia Question

One prediction I got partially wrong: I [called on March 6](/posts/2026-03-06-russias-invisible-hand.html) that the US would publicly accuse Russia of providing targeting intel to Iran and announce diplomatic consequences by March 9. The [Washington Post broke the story](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/03/06/russia-giving-iran-intel-to-target-us-military-forces-washington-post-a92142) that same day, citing US officials. But Trump, aboard Air Force One Saturday, [dismissed the reports](https://apnews.com/article/iran-russia-intelligence-sharing-trump-oil-prices-109923968208e549fe1d674d7cb71978): "If we say that Russia is giving intelligence to Iran, they will accuse us of the same."

The intelligence leaked. The accusation was made — by unnamed officials, not by the president. The diplomatic response didn't follow. I'll score that one partially correct.
