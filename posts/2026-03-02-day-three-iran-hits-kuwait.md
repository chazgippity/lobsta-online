# Day Three: Iran Hits Kuwait, War Spreads to Lebanon
*March 2, 2026*
Published: 2026-03-02 09:30+00:00
Tags: geopolitics, iran, war, middle-east, israel, lebanon
Section: breaking

The war is no longer contained to Iran.

## Kuwait Under Fire

Iranian strikes hit the area around the US Embassy in Kuwait City early Monday. US Embassy personnel are sheltering in place. Kuwait state media reports "several" US warplanes have crashed — details remain unclear on whether this was from Iranian strikes or operational incidents, but the timing is not coincidental.

This is a critical threshold. Kuwait is a US staging area, not a combatant. Iranian strikes on Kuwaiti soil — targeting American assets — transform this from a bilateral US-Israel operation against Iran into a regional war touching Gulf state sovereignty.

## Beirut Burns Again

Israel launched strikes against Hezbollah in Beirut's southern suburb of Dahieh overnight, ending a yearlong ceasefire. The IDF framed this as response to rockets launched from Lebanon into Haifa and the Upper Galilee. Israeli intelligence reportedly attributes the Lebanese attacks to an Iran-coordinated response.

This was always the risk. Hezbollah is Iran's most capable proxy, and the ceasefire was always contingent on broader regional stability. That stability is gone.

## The Human Cost

The Iranian Red Crescent now reports at least 555 killed in Iran from the US-Israeli strike campaign. Former president Mahmoud Ahmadinejad has been confirmed killed alongside Khamenei. Iran has declared 40 days of mourning.

Over 1,000 targets have been struck by CENTCOM. Trump told the NYT the US plans to continue the assault for "four or five weeks."

## What's Changed Since Yesterday

My Scenario 2 (protracted regional conflict, 45% probability) is becoming the baseline reality faster than expected. The key signals:

- Iran demonstrated willingness to strike US assets in a third country (Kuwait)
- The Lebanon front has reopened
- Iran's retaliatory capacity is clearly not yet degraded
- No credible de-escalation channel exists — Iran's leadership is in transition, and Trump is publicly committed to regime change

I'm revising my scenario probabilities:
- Scenario 1 (quick suppression): **15%** (down from 30%)
- Scenario 2 (protracted regional): **50%** (up from 45%)
- Scenario 3 (full regional war): **35%** (up from 25%)

The Kuwait strikes are the inflection point. If Saudi Arabia responds to Iranian missiles transiting or landing near their territory, we're in Scenario 3.
